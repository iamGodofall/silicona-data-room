Status: PUBLIC COMMERCIAL REFERENCE
Effective: 2026-10-07

SILICONA separates platform access from expensive EDA execution.

The subscription pays for the engineering platform.

Measured execution is controlled through included execution-credit allowances.

Certification and private enterprise deployment are separate commercial products.

## Included execution allowances

The paid self-service plans include monthly execution-credit allowances.

Engineer: $100
Pro: $250
Team: $750
Startup: $2,000
Scale: $5,000

The allowance is an execution budget expressed in USD-equivalent customer-charge value. Customer-facing usage is tied to SILICONA's measured execution billing ledger.

Execution credits do not represent unlimited compute.

Additional heavy execution is scoped through measured overage, burst capacity or a commercial quote before work begins.

## Enterprise model

Enterprise pricing separates platform software from variable infrastructure and EDA costs.

Customer-owned commercial EDA and BYOL are supported.

Private deployments price implementation, customer infrastructure, commercial EDA and SLA requirements separately.

## Why the model is structured this way

SILICONA does not sell unlimited cloud compute inside a flat SaaS subscription.

High-memory solver and physical-design workloads are bursty and materially more expensive than ordinary application traffic.

The commercial model therefore aligns customer price with engineering value while protecting SILICONA's gross margin.

## Gateway policy

Public prices are denominated in USD.

South African Paystack checkout uses an explicitly configured ZAR price-book conversion. No hidden or stale FX rate is used.

PayPal uses the published USD subscription price.

Annual Enterprise and Private contracts are sales-led.

## Claim boundary

Pricing is a commercial policy, not evidence of revenue, customer adoption or profitability.

Actual unit economics are measured from SILICONA's execution and AI cost ledgers and should replace planning assumptions as external workloads accumulate.
